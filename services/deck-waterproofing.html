Deck waterproofing is an installed system — a pedestrian-rated coating over a properly prepared surface, flashing at every joint and penetration, and drainage that carries water away — not a product you roll on over a problem. That distinction is the whole page. Most searches for deck waterproofing land on membrane rolls, flashing tape and spray cans, and those products have real uses; they just do not fix a deck that is already letting water into the framing or into the room below. If you have tried one and the stain came back, you are in the right place: we install the system and we find the leak first.

We are a repair company, not a coatings dealer. That changes the order of operations. We do not quote a coating until we know where the water is going, because waterproofing over soft wood seals the rot in and hides it from you for another two years.

If you have a rooftop deck, start here instead

Most of the waterproofing people actually hire for in Baltimore is rooftop work, and it is a different discipline. Go to rowhome rooftop deck repair and rooftop deck waterproofing — on a roof deck, the waterproofing is the roof, and getting it wrong puts water in the bedroom below.

Do I need waterproofing, or just sealing?

Answer first: sealing is a finish, waterproofing is a system. The deciding question is what is underneath the deck.

If nothing sits under your deck and the framing is dry, you probably want deck staining and sealing and you will save money. We will tell you that on the estimate rather than selling you the bigger job.

Signs your deck has a water problem, not a finish problem

  • Stains, drips or peeling paint on a ceiling, soffit or wall under or behind the deck
  • Soft or spongy wood at the ledger — the board bolting the deck to the house, and the most failure-prone component on any deck
  • Rust streaks and black stains around fasteners and hardware, which mean the wood around them stays wet
  • A gap opening between the deck and the house, or any sway when you walk
  • Standing water that sits on the boards for hours after rain, or a surface that never fully dries
  • Efflorescence or damp on masonry or concrete under the deck
  • Soft post bases where a post meets concrete without the 1-inch standoff the code requires for weather- and splash-exposed post bases

Two of those point straight at flashing. The code requires continuous flashing at the ledger-to-house connection (2021 IRC R507.9.1.4), and a great many decks in this market predate that being taken seriously — decks from the 1980s and 1990s were routinely nailed to the house with no flashing at all. Here is what that failure looks like and why the ledger is where decks fail first.

How does the job run, step by step?

  1. Diagnose before quoting. We trace where the water is entering — field, edge, flashing joint or penetration — and check the ledger, framing, post bases and hardware. Free, and in writing.
  2. Repair the structure. Rotted boards and framing out, hardware upgraded, flashing corrected. Nothing gets coated over. If the framing needs real work, that is structural and rot repair and it happens first.
  3. Prepare the substrate. Clean, sound, dry. This is where most coating failures are born — a coating on damp or dirty substrate debonds no matter how good the product is.
  4. Detail the edges and penetrations. Flashing, edge metal, curbs and post bases. Water almost never comes through the middle of a properly coated field; it comes in at a detail.
  5. Apply the system. Coating or membrane installed to the manufacturer's build, in weather that allows it to cure.
  6. Handle drainage. Water that is now being shed has to go somewhere — that means drainage, downspout tie-in or a slope correction, not a puddle against the house.
  7. Walkthrough and warranty. We show you what was done, where the vulnerable details are, and what to watch.

Why Baltimore-area decks need this more than most

  • Roughly 45 inches of rain a year — the sum of the NWS BWI 1991–2020 monthly normals.
  • Winter that swings above and below freezing repeatedly, prying open every joint that holds water, plus 19.3 inches of average annual snowfall.
  • July average highs near 88.8°F and January average lows near 25.4°F — that thermal range moves an assembly all year.
  • Humidity near 75% from August through October, so wet things stay wet.
  • Storms. NOAA NCEI counts 85 billion-dollar disasters affecting Maryland from 1980 to 2024, and the recent-year average is 5.6 events annually against 1.9 historically. Hurricane Isabel in 2003 pushed a tide 8.34 feet above mean high water at Baltimore; the remnants of Ida dropped 2 to 5 inches of rain across northern Maryland in September 2021.

Wood takes the brunt of it. Pressure-treated southern yellow pine gives roughly 10 to 15 years with maintenance and under 10 when it is never sealed — the difference is almost entirely water management.

Why hire a pro rather than buying a product?

  • Diagnosis is the job. A coating applied to the wrong surface fixes nothing. The leak is usually at a flashing joint, a penetration or an edge, and finding it is what you are paying for.
  • Substrate prep decides the outcome. Damp, dirty or unsound substrate is why most consumer coatings peel within a year.
  • Pedestrian-rated products matter. Deck surfaces take abrasion, point loads and thermal movement. Products built for roofs or patches crack under foot traffic.
  • Rot has to come out first. We are repair specialists, so we can actually do that part instead of coating over it.
  • Local weather scheduling. Coatings need a cure window. With 30 thunderstorm days a year in eastern Maryland, peaking in July, the forecast sets the date.
  • Insured and bonded, free written estimates, workmanship warranty.

Rooftop decks: where waterproofing really earns its money

On a Baltimore rowhome, the deck is on the roof and the room below is somebody's bedroom. Membrane failure is the number one problem on those decks — blistering, alligatoring and cracking from UV and thermal movement — and a standard roofing membrane installed where a pedestrian deck waterproofing system belongs cracks within a couple of seasons. Every penetration, post base and curb has to be flashed. And the hard one: once a deck sits on a roof, replacing the membrane under it requires removing the deck, which is why doing it right the first time is worth so much.

Baltimore City rowhome rooftop decks also carry their own code path — no exemption from permitting, construction documents signed and sealed by a Maryland architect or engineer, and CHAP approval before the building permit in historic districts like Fells Point and Federal Hill. Deck Waterproofing FAQs

What is the difference between sealing a deck and waterproofing it?

Sealing is a finish; waterproofing is a system. A sealer or stain soaks into the wood and slows absorption, and it needs reapplication every 12 to 18 months — sometimes every 6 to 12 months on a deck in full sun with heavy traffic. Waterproofing means building an assembly that keeps water from reaching the structure at all: a pedestrian-rated coating or membrane over a prepared substrate, flashing at every joint and penetration, and drainage that takes the water somewhere. If your goal is colour and a nicer-looking deck, you want staining and sealing. If your goal is a dry space under the deck or a ceiling that stops staining, you want waterproofing.

Can a waterproofing product from the hardware store fix my deck?

For a leak, almost never. Membrane rolls, flashing tape, elastomeric paint and spray-on coatings all have legitimate uses, but they fail when they are applied over a problem rather than to a prepared assembly. The three reasons they do not hold: the substrate was damp or dirty when the product went on, the leak was actually at a flashing joint or penetration the coating never touched, and the product was not rated for foot traffic. A pedestrian surface takes abrasion, point loads from furniture, and thermal movement that a roofing or patch product is not built for. If you have already tried a product and the stain came back, that is diagnostic information rather than a wasted effort — it usually means the water is entering at a detail, not through the field.

How much does deck waterproofing cost?

No Maryland-specific published pricing exists for deck work, so these are national averages. Nationally, waterproofing runs $2.30 to $5.40 per square foot. Related line items: sealing alone $0.75 to $4 per square foot or $550 to $1,300 for a project, flashing $15 to $30 per linear foot, and refinishing $3 to $6.50 per square foot. Do I need waterproofing or just a repair?

Find out where the water is going first. If rain lands on the deck and drains away and the framing is dry, you need a finish, not a system. If there is living space, a patio, a garage or a ceiling under the deck, or if you are seeing stains, drips, rusting hardware or soft wood at the ledger, then water is being held against the structure and a coating alone will not solve it. We inspect the ledger, flashing, post bases, fasteners and framing before recommending anything, because waterproofing over rot traps the rot.

Why do rooftop decks need a different waterproofing system?

Because on a rooftop deck the waterproofing is the roof. A backyard deck sheds water to the ground; a Baltimore rowhome rooftop deck sits on the roof surface of an occupied house, so any failure lands in the room below. Membrane failure is the number one problem we see on those decks — blistering, alligatoring, and cracking from UV and thermal movement — and every penetration, post base and curb has to be flashed. A standard roofing membrane installed where a pedestrian deck waterproofing system belongs cracks within a couple of seasons. The other hard fact: once a deck sits on a roof, replacing the membrane underneath it means removing the deck.

Do I need a permit for deck waterproofing in Maryland?

Sealing, staining and power washing are not addressed in any local county's permit materials, and surface coating work generally sits in the same category. What does trigger permitting is structural work uncovered along the way, and the rules differ at every county line. Baltimore County requires a permit for decks more than 16 inches above grade or larger than 120 square feet. Howard and Anne Arundel counties treat board-only replacement as ordinary repair. Baltimore City has no deck exemption at all, and rooftop decks there carry their own requirements, including construction documents signed and sealed by a Maryland architect or engineer. We tell you what applies to your address before work begins.

How long does deck waterproofing last?

Longer than a stain, and the number depends on the system and the exposure rather than on a single product claim. What shortens it in this climate is well documented: Baltimore gets roughly 45 inches of rain a year, swings above and below freezing repeatedly through the winter, and sees July average highs near 89 degrees, so an assembly expands, contracts and takes water every year of its life. The details are what age first — flashing joints, post-base penetrations and edges — which is why we inspect those rather than just recoating the field. We will also tell you plainly when a deck needs framing repair before it needs a new coating.
