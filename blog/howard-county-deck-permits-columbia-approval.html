Deck Permits in Howard County and Columbia's Village Approval Process

Howard County exempts almost nothing by height — and in Columbia, the county permit is only half the paperwork.

Home › Blog › Howard County Deck Permits & Columbia Village Approval

In Howard County, only decks under 25 square feet are exempt from a building permit — and there is no height exemption at all. Board-only replacement is the one repair the county explicitly treats as ordinary repair with no permit. And if your address is in Columbia, the county permit is only half of it: every property there sits under recorded covenants, and all decks require an application to your village architectural committee before construction begins.

That combination catches people out constantly. A homeowner reads a national article about a 30-inch height rule and concludes a low deck needs nothing filed — but height releases you from nothing here. And a Columbia homeowner who pulls a clean county permit can still be stopped by a covenant advisor, because the village never saw a drawing.

When does Howard County require a deck permit?

The test is area, not height. Anything 25 square feet or larger needs a permit, whatever it sits on and however close to the ground it is.

Why Howard's rule surprises homeowners who moved from Baltimore County

Baltimore County's trigger is a height figure and an area figure: more than 16 inches above grade, or more than 120 square feet. Howard County has no height figure whatsoever and sets its area figure at 25 square feet — nearly five times stricter. Two counties, twenty minutes apart, two completely different answers. If you own in Ellicott City and your parents own in Timonium, do not trade permit advice across the dinner table. Here is how Baltimore County's rule actually reads.

Which Howard County deck repairs don't need a permit?

Howard County is one of only two jurisdictions in this metro that put board-only replacement in writing as exempt ordinary repair — useful, because resurfacing is the most common deck job we quote.

  • Replacing decking boards only — exempt as ordinary repair. The framing underneath stays as it is.
  • Refinishing — power washing, stripping, sanding, staining and sealing are not addressed in any Baltimore-area county's permit materials, Howard's included. That is not the same as a published exemption, but finish work does not touch structure.
  • Like-for-like hardware — replacing corroded fasteners or a failed post base with a properly rated equivalent.

Once the scope reaches joists, beams, posts, footings, guards, stairs or the ledger, it is structural. That is where a permit and the inspection sequence come in, and it is the dividing line we walk through at the estimate before any structural and rot repair starts. A deck board replacement job turns into a framing job more often than people expect, because the soft boards were soft for a reason.

What inspections does Howard County require?

  1. Footing inspection — holes open, before concrete is placed. Once it is poured, nobody can verify the depth.
  2. Framing inspection — structure framed, ledger attached, hardware visible, before decking goes down.
  3. Final inspection — the completed deck including guards and stairs.

The sequence is a map of what actually holds a deck up: what is in the ground, and what connects the frame to the house. Both are invisible the moment the job is finished, which is exactly why they get inspected while they are open.

What building code does Howard County use for decks?

Howard County has adopted the 2024 International Residential Code. Baltimore County, Anne Arundel, Harford and Carroll are working from the 2021 edition. That divergence is not academic — span tables, connection details and hardware requirements are exactly the kind of thing that moves between editions.

County deck handouts age faster than the code. Printed and PDF deck guides are often published under an older edition than the one currently enforced. Use them for orientation, then confirm current span tables and connection details directly with Howard County before anyone cuts lumber. This is the single cheapest phone call in the whole project.

Do you need village approval for a deck in Columbia?

Yes — and this is the part that has no equivalent anywhere else in the metro. Every property in Columbia is subject to recorded covenants. Those covenants are administered not by one central office but by the architectural committee of the village the property sits in. All decks require an application before construction.

There are ten villages, each developed in its own era, each with its own committee, forms and meeting rhythm:

Those founding years are a decent proxy for deck age. A Wilde Lake or Harper's Choice house is close to sixty years old, so any deck on it is at least a second-generation structure — and even River Hill's original decks are now old enough for fasteners, ledgers and framing to start reporting in.

What does a village architectural committee want to see?

Wilde Lake publishes its submission requirements, and they are a good model for what any village committee is weighing:

  • A site plan showing the deck's position relative to the house, the lot lines and neighboring properties.
  • A dimensioned drawing showing style, height above grade, and railing and stair details.
  • Documentation of any window or door changes — a new slider to reach the deck is part of the application.
  • A material and color description.
  • A screening and landscaping drawing.
  • Exterior lighting details.

The standards behind that list matter as much as the list. Work must match the house in scale, materials, color and texture. And a deck may not impair a neighbor's view, sunlight or ventilation, or change drainage — which is why height above grade and the position relative to the lot line get the attention they do on a sloped Columbia lot.

How long does the approval take, and who do you call?

Hickory Ridge publishes its sequence, and it is the clearest picture available of how the process runs:

  1. Submit an Exterior Alteration Application.
  2. The application goes to a Covenant Advisor.
  3. The Resident Architectural Committee makes a property visit.
  4. The decision arrives by email — typically two to four weeks.

Hickory Ridge's Covenant Advisor line is 410-730-7327. Other villages run their own timelines and contacts, so start with your village office rather than assuming Hickory Ridge's schedule is universal.

What if the committee turns the application down?

A denial is not the end of the road. Escalation runs to the Columbia Association Architectural Resource Committee by way of a 15-Day Notice filed through the village board. Before going that route, ask what specifically failed — most denials trace back to something the application simply did not document.

Is there a faster track for replacing a deck as-is?

In Town Center, yes. Town Center operates a "Like for Like" streamlined review in 2026 for work that reproduces what is already there. If your project genuinely replaces an existing deck in the same footprint, materials and colors, ask whether it qualifies before filing the long-form application.

Where more than one set of covenants applies, the most restrictive governs. Some Columbia properties sit under a village covenant and an additional neighborhood or association covenant. They do not average out and you do not get to pick — the strictest applicable standard is the one that controls the project.

One thing we won't guess at: re-staining in the existing color. Village guidelines plainly require an application for a new deck and for changes to material or color. Whether re-staining a deck in the color it already is triggers an application is not something we can confirm from published village materials, so we are not going to state it either way. Confirm it with your village Covenant Advisor before the work starts. We ask that question for Columbia customers as a matter of course before we book a staining and sealing or full deck restoration job.

Howard County and Columbia deck approval FAQs

Does a low deck need a permit in Howard County?

Almost certainly yes. Howard County's only exemption is size: decks under 25 square feet. There is no height exemption at all, so a deck sitting a few inches off the ground still needs a permit once it is 25 square feet or larger. That is a different test from Baltimore County, which exempts decks that are both 16 inches or less above grade and 120 square feet or smaller.

Do I need a permit to replace deck boards in Howard County?

No. Howard County treats board-only replacement as ordinary repair, which does not require a permit. That exemption covers the decking surface only. The moment the work reaches joists, beams, posts, footings or the ledger, it is structural, and structural work is what the permit and inspection process is built around.

How deep do deck footings have to be in Howard County?

Howard County requires deck footings at a 30-inch frost depth. That is the depth a footing has to reach to sit below the level where the ground freezes and heaves, which is what keeps a deck from lifting and racking over successive winters. Stair landings and stringer supports are footings too, even though they are the ones most often set on soil or a patio block instead.

What code does Howard County use for decks?

Howard County has moved to the 2024 International Residential Code, while neighboring Baltimore, Anne Arundel, Harford and Carroll counties are working from the 2021 edition. A deck in Ellicott City and a deck ten minutes away over the county line are being measured against different books, so span tables, details and hardware requirements printed in older county handouts should be confirmed with Howard County before lumber is cut.

Do I need village approval for a deck in Columbia, Maryland?

Yes. Every property in Columbia is subject to recorded covenants, and all decks require an application before construction. Columbia does not run this through one central office — each of the ten villages has its own architectural committee, so the forms, the meeting schedule and the emphasis vary by village. The county building permit and the village approval are two separate approvals, and you need both.

How long does Columbia village deck approval take?

In Hickory Ridge, the published sequence is an Exterior Alteration Application, then review with a Covenant Advisor, then a property visit by the Resident Architectural Committee, then a decision by email — typically two to four weeks. Other villages run their own timelines. Build the review window into your project schedule rather than treating it as a formality at the end.

What happens if the village architectural committee denies my deck?

A denial can be escalated to the Columbia Association Architectural Resource Committee through a 15-Day Notice filed by way of the village board. It is worth asking your Covenant Advisor what specifically failed first, because most denials come down to a detail the application did not document clearly — height above grade, a material or color that does not match the house, or an effect on a neighbor's view, light or drainage.

Do I need an application to re-stain my Columbia deck in the same color?

We are not going to guess at this one. Village guidelines clearly require an application for a new deck and for changes to materials and colors, but whether re-staining in the existing color needs an application is not something we can confirm from published village materials. Ask your village Covenant Advisor before the work starts — a two-minute phone call settles it.
