Crofton is the flattest market we serve, and that is genuinely good news for your wallet. Built from 1964 on the former Duvall farm, Crofton sits on level ground — which means fewer walkout basements, fewer multi-level decks standing a story above the yard, and far less of the height-driven structural work that dominates our hillside jobs. Most Crofton deck repair is surface, railing, stair and finish work. We would rather say that up front than talk you into something bigger.

What flat ground changes about a deck

Crofton runs a median household income around $154,592 and a median home value near $540,345, on former farmland with a 3.5-mile loop, Crofton Parkway, running through it. Decks here typically sit low and close to the yard. Three practical consequences follow:

  • Less height-driven structure to fail. Guards are required over 30 inches above grade, at least 36 inches high on a house, with no gap a 4-inch sphere can pass through — but a lot of Crofton decks sit below or barely above that line, so guard and lateral-load work is a smaller share of the job than it is in the hillier parts of the metro.
  • Shorter stairs, but the same failure point. Two or three risers instead of a full flight still means stringers and stair footings, and stringers resting directly on soil or a dropped block rot from the bottom up with no visible warning until a tread gives.
  • Low decks trap moisture. A deck close to grade ventilates poorly underneath. The code requires a one-inch standoff above concrete for weather- and splash-exposed post bases, and wood within six inches of exposed ground must be ground-contact rated — details that get skipped often and matter most exactly where the deck sits low.

So the honest summary: in Crofton we do more deck board replacement, railing repair and refinishing than framing rebuilds. We still inspect the ledger, joists, posts and footings on every visit, because a low deck hides rot better than a tall one does.

Sun, rain and the finish cycle

Without the heavy canopy you get closer to the water, Crofton decks take more direct sun — and the USDA Forest Products Laboratory documents that wood is rapidly degraded by short-wavelength ultraviolet light, with further subsurface degradation from longer-wavelength violet and visible light. Sun, not shade, sets the refinishing clock here.

The rest of the weather still applies. The area gets roughly 45 inches of rain a year — the sum of the National Weather Service's 1991–2020 monthly normals at BWI — humidity near 75% from August through October, and temperatures that swing above and below freezing repeatedly through the winter, prying open every joint a little further each season. Horizontal boards hold standing water while vertical faces shed it, so the walking surface is always the first thing to go. As a baseline, water-repellent sealers generally need reapplication every 12 to 18 months, and solid-color finishes last one to three years on horizontals versus three to seven on vertical faces. Timing the work to Maryland's weather matters as much as the product you choose.

Approvals in Crofton: two different systems

Crofton's governance is split, and it catches homeowners out. The original 1964 planned community section is a Special Community Benefit District. The newer sections that filled in around it have individual homeowners associations. Those are different structures with different rules about exterior work, so there is no single Crofton answer about approval — check your own deed and association documents before scheduling. We will tell you what the county requires; what your association requires is set at that level.

Anne Arundel County permit rules

  • An attached deck always requires a permit — regardless of size or height. Anne Arundel is the only county around Baltimore that draws this distinction, and on Crofton's low decks it is the rule that surprises people most.
  • A detached deck is exempt only if it is under 200 square feet and under 3 feet high and outside the Critical Area — all three at once.
  • Board-only replacement is exempt as ordinary repair, which covers a good share of Crofton work.
  • Inspections run footing, framing and final, under the 2021 International Residential Code.
  • Staining, sealing and power washing are not addressed in the county's permit materials at all — not the same thing as "no permit needed."

Deck Repair FAQs for Crofton Homeowners

Do I need community or HOA approval for deck work in Crofton?

It depends which part of Crofton you are in, and that surprises people. The original 1964 planned community section is a Special Community Benefit District, while the newer sections that filled in around it have their own individual homeowners associations. Those are different governing structures with different rules about exterior work, so there is no single Crofton answer. Check your own deed and association documents before scheduling anything, because approval requirements are set at that level rather than by the county.

Do I need a county permit to repair a deck in Crofton?

Anne Arundel County is the only county around Baltimore that separates attached from detached decks. An attached deck always requires a permit, regardless of size or height. A detached deck is exempt only if it is under 200 square feet AND under 3 feet high AND outside the Critical Area — all three together. Board-only replacement is exempt as ordinary repair, which covers a lot of the work we do in Crofton. Inspections run footing, framing and final under the 2021 International Residential Code.

My deck is low to the ground because the lot is flat. Doesn't that mean no permit?

Not if it is attached to the house. Crofton's ground is flat former farmland, so decks here often sit low — but the height and size exemption in Anne Arundel County applies only to detached structures. A low attached deck still requires a permit. Being close to the ground does change the repair itself though: low decks trap moisture underneath, ventilate poorly, and put framing and post bases in constant contact with damp soil.

What actually needs repairing on a Crofton deck?

Mostly surfaces, railings, stairs and finish rather than major structural rebuilds. Without the walkout basements and tall multi-level decks you get on hillside lots elsewhere in the metro, Crofton decks carry less height-driven structural load — so the work concentrates on cracked and cupped boards, popped fasteners, loose railings, worn treads and failed finish. That does not mean we skip the framing: we still check the ledger, joists, posts and footings on every visit, because low decks hide rot better than tall ones. A documented inspection is the cheapest way to find out which you have.

How often should a Crofton deck be refinished?

Plan on a cycle rather than a one-off. Water-repellent sealers generally need reapplication every 12 to 18 months, sometimes every 6 to 12 under sun and traffic; semi-transparent stains run 12 to 18 months on horizontal surfaces; solid-color finishes last one to three years on horizontals versus three to seven on vertical faces. Crofton's decks get more direct sun than the canopy-covered parts of the county, and short-wavelength ultraviolet light degrades exposed wood rapidly, so sun exposure drives the schedule here more than shade does. Staining and sealing on that cycle is what keeps the boards out of the repair queue.
