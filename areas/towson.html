The Deck Repair Wizard repairs decks, porches, railings and stairs in Towson, Maryland — about ten minutes south of our base in Lutherville-Timonium. What makes Towson different from every other town we cover is that it isn't one housing market. It's a county seat of roughly 59,533 people where only 33.7% of homes are single-family detached, and where the right answer to "can you fix my outdoor space?" depends entirely on which side of York Road you live on.

Where the decks are in Towson — and where they aren't

The detached-home neighborhoods are the deck market. Anneslie, Idlewylde, Stoneleigh, Knollwood-Donnybrook, Campus Hills, West Towson and Loch Raven Village are where we find raised wood decks off the back of the house, carried on posts, with stairs down to a sloping yard. Those decks were overwhelmingly added after the houses were built, which puts them in the same 1980s-through-2000s wave as the rest of north Baltimore County — old enough that the hardware, the ledger detail and the finish are all due, even when the boards still look passable.

Rodgers Forge and Wiltondale are a different story, and we'd rather say so than sell you something. These are 1930s to 1950s brick, rowhouse-style blocks. The outdoor space at these houses is typically a porch, a set of steps and a patio — not a raised deck. If you live in the Forge, what usually needs the work is porch framing that's gone soft, a step that has separated, a handrail with no real holding power, or a post base sitting where water collects. That is still our work; it's the same failure mechanism — water at the connections and wood in contact with masonry or soil — on a different structure. We just don't pretend it's a deck job.

The brick-veneer problem. Towson has a lot of brick-faced houses, and the code is blunt about this: a deck ledger may not be attached to brick or stone veneer, because veneer is non-structural. It's a facing, not a load path. Cantilevered floor joists and engineered I-joist rims without manufacturer reinforcement are off-limits too. When we find a deck hanging off veneer, the fix is either a correct connection through to real structure or a free-standing deck on its own posts and footings. What Towson weather and terrain do to these decks

Towson sits where the yards start to fall away toward the Loch Raven watershed, which is why so many decks here are three, four or more feet above grade on the downhill side — and why stairs and guards matter so much locally. Height converts an ordinary maintenance item into a safety item.

The climate does the slow damage. Roughly 45 inches of rain a year (the sum of the National Weather Service's 1991–2020 monthly normals at BWI), about 19.3 inches of snow, and a winter that swings above and below freezing repeatedly — every cycle drives water into a joint and levers it open a little further. Humidity near 75% from August through October keeps shaded boards damp long enough for algae and mildew to take hold, and a freeze-free season of more than 230 days gives rot most of the year to work. Horizontal decking holds standing water; vertical railings and skirting shed it. That's why the walking surface is almost always the first thing to go.

Baltimore County permit rules in Towson

Towson is unincorporated Baltimore County, so county rules govern — and they're stricter than the national rule of thumb almost everyone repeats.

  • Permit required over 16 inches above grade, or over 120 square feet. Not 30 inches. Sixteen. A deck two steps down from a back door is already over the line, and 120 square feet is a 10-by-12.
  • No exemption in a Historic District, on the Landmarks List, in a floodplain or in the Critical Area.
  • Three inspections: footing, framing, completion — framing before the decking covers it up.
  • Baltimore County adopted the 2021 IRC effective September 3, 2024.
  • Unlike Howard County, Baltimore County does not publish a board-only-replacement exemption, so we don't assume one exists.
  • Staining, sealing and power washing are not addressed in any county's permit materials — that's the accurate statement, not "no permit needed."

Where the code bites on repairs: guards are required above 30 inches of drop and must be at least 36 inches high on a one- or two-family home, with no opening a 4-inch sphere can pass through. Stairs run to a maximum 7¾-inch riser and a minimum 10-inch tread, with a graspable handrail 34 to 38 inches high required at four or more risers. Footings go to a 30-inch frost depth. Local Deck & Porch Questions

Which Towson neighborhoods actually have decks?

Only 33.7 percent of Towson housing is single-family detached, so the deck market is concentrated in the detached-home neighborhoods: Anneslie, Idlewylde, Stoneleigh, Knollwood-Donnybrook, Campus Hills, West Towson and Loch Raven Village. Rodgers Forge and Wiltondale are a different animal — 1930s to 1950s brick rowhouse-style housing where the outdoor space is usually a porch, steps and a patio rather than a raised wood deck. We work in both, but the job is not the same job.

Can my deck ledger be bolted to a brick wall in Towson?

Not to brick or stone veneer. The code prohibits attaching a deck ledger to veneer because veneer is non-structural — it is a facing, not a load path. On a brick-faced Towson house the deck either has to be attached through to the actual structure with a detail that works, or built free-standing on its own posts and footings. Cantilevered floor joists and engineered I-joist rims without manufacturer reinforcement are also off the table. This is one of the most common things we find done wrong on older Towson decks.

Do you repair front porches and porch steps in Towson, not just decks?

Yes. In the rowhouse-style blocks the structure that needs help is usually a porch: soft framing under the porch floor, steps that have pulled away, loose handrails and posts sitting on masonry that has stopped shedding water. The failure modes are the same ones we chase on decks — water at the connections and wood in contact with the ground — so the repairs are the same work on a different structure.

Does Towson follow Baltimore County deck permit rules?

Yes. Towson is unincorporated Baltimore County, so the county threshold applies: a permit is required for a deck more than 16 inches above grade or larger than 120 square feet, with no exemption in a Historic District, on the Landmarks List, in a floodplain or in the Critical Area. Inspections are footing, framing and completion. Staining, sealing and power washing are not addressed in the county permit materials.

My railing wobbles. Is that a repair or a rebuild?

Usually a repair, and usually at the posts rather than the rail. Guard posts loosen because the fasteners have corroded, the post base has rotted, or the post was attached in a way that never had much holding power. While it is apart we bring the guard up to current requirements: at least 36 inches high on a one- or two-family home, with no opening a 4-inch sphere can pass through. A wobbly railing on a deck three or four feet above a sloped yard is the single most urgent item we get called about in Towson.
