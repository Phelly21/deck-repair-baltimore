Fallston is, by the numbers, the most deck-dense place in the entire Baltimore metro: 99.4% of its homes are single-family detached, and the median one was built in 1977. Almost every house here has a back yard, a rear door and, in most cases, a deck off the back of it that is now somewhere between thirty and forty-five years old. The Deck Repair Wizard repairs those decks — rotted framing, loose railings, soft boards, tired finish — and tells you honestly when one is genuinely past saving.

Fallston's housing stock is why so many decks come due at once

Most communities are a mix. Fallston is not. At 99.4% detached, there is essentially no townhome or apartment stock diluting the picture — which means the deck-per-household rate here is about as high as it gets in this market. Pair that with a median build year of 1977 and the timeline writes itself: the houses went up in the 1970s, the decks were added on through the 1980s and 1990s, and they are all arriving at the same place at the same time.

That place is predictable. The Southern Pine Council treats pressure-treated southern yellow pine as a 10-to-15-year material with maintenance, stretching toward 15 to 25 years above ground when every field cut is resealed and dropping under 10 when a deck is never sealed at all. A 1980s Fallston deck has usually been through two or three finish cycles and at least one long stretch with no finish at all. Meanwhile the connections have been quietly working loose. Decks of that era were routinely fastened to the house with nails instead of bolts and no continuous flashing behind the ledger, which is exactly the failure pattern that makes the ledger the most failure-prone component on a deck. Here is what a failing nailed ledger looks like and how to check yours from the ground.

Fallston households have also tended to invest in the deck rather than remove it — median household income here runs $154,688 against a median home value of $562,300. That usually means a substantial structure: a two-level deck, a wraparound, built-in benches, a privacy wall, sometimes a screened section. Those are worth repairing. They are also the decks a replacement-focused contractor most wants to tear down.

Rolling Piedmont terrain and Maryland weather

Fallston sits on rolling Piedmont ground, and that shows up in the framing. Decks here step down a grade on tall posts far more often than they sit a step off level lawn. Height changes the repair: more leverage at every post-to-beam and post-to-footing connection, longer stair runs, and guards that are unambiguously in code territory rather than borderline. The code requires guards where a walking surface is more than 30 inches above grade, a minimum 36-inch guard height on one- and two-family homes, and no opening a 4-inch sphere can pass through.

The weather does the slow damage. The Baltimore area takes roughly 45 inches of rain a year — the sum of the National Weather Service's 1991–2020 monthly normals at BWI — and swings above and below freezing repeatedly through the winter, so water works into every joint, freezes and pries it a little wider each season. Humidity sits near 75% from August through October, which is when the shaded half of a Fallston deck grows algae while the sunny half fades. And with a freeze-free season of more than 230 days, there is a long window for decay to progress. Horizontal boards hold standing water while vertical faces shed it — that is why the walking surface fails first and the railing posts fail quietly underneath.

What we repair on Fallston decks

  • Structural and rot repair — rotted joists, a failing ledger, sinking posts, sagging beams. On tall Fallston decks this is the work that decides whether the deck is safe to stand on.

Harford County deck permit rules

Harford County does not publish a numeric exemption for decks. Where Baltimore County names a specific trigger — more than 16 inches above grade or larger than 120 square feet — Harford's materials instead state that permits are required for all new structures, listing sheds, decks and additions by name. There is no size threshold to hide behind and no published height cutoff.

The practical points for a Fallston homeowner:

  • Assume permit-work. The 30-inch rule people repeat from national sites has no basis in Harford's published materials. Start from the assumption that a deck project needs a permit and confirm with the county from there.
  • Code basis: the 2021 International Residential Code. That is the rulebook your repair will be measured against — guards, stair geometry, ledger fastening, footing depth.
  • Three inspections: footing, then framing, then final. The footing visit has to happen while the holes are still open, which is why new-post and new-footing work gets sequenced into the schedule rather than added at the end.
  • Staining, sealing and power washing are not addressed in Harford's permit materials — which is not the same thing as "no permit needed." We say it that way on purpose.

Harford is stricter in shape than Baltimore County even though Baltimore County's number is famously low. For the contrast, our Baltimore County deck permit guide lays out the 16-inch and 120-square-foot triggers and why so many homeowners get them wrong.

Deck Repair FAQs for Fallston Homeowners

Do I need a permit to repair a deck in Fallston?

Fallston is in unincorporated Harford County, and Harford's permit materials do not publish a size or height exemption for decks the way Baltimore County does. The county states that permits are required for all new structures, and names sheds, decks and additions specifically. In practice that means you should assume a deck project in Fallston is permit-work until the county tells you otherwise, and it also means you cannot rely on the 30-inch rule homeowners repeat from national websites. We tell you what your specific scope triggers before we start, and we will say plainly when a repair is small enough that it is worth calling the county to confirm.

What inspections does Harford County require on deck work?

Harford County runs a three-stage sequence: a footing inspection before concrete is placed, a framing inspection once the structure is built but before it is covered, and a final inspection when the work is complete. Harford is working from the 2021 International Residential Code. The footing stage is the one homeowners lose time on, because it has to happen while the holes are open — so if your Fallston repair involves new posts or footings, that visit gets scheduled into the job rather than bolted on afterward.

Why do so many Fallston decks need work at the same time?

Because the housing stock aged together. Fallston is 99.4% single-family detached homes with a median build year of 1977. Decks on houses of that vintage were typically added in the 1980s and 1990s, which puts a very large share of Fallston decks in the 30-to-45-year bracket. That is precisely the age where pressure-treated southern yellow pine reaches the end of the 10-to-15-year service life the Southern Pine Council describes for maintained decks, where nailed ledger connections start pulling, and where the original finish has long since stopped shedding water.

My Fallston deck sits on a slope. Does that change the repair?

It changes almost everything about it. Fallston sits on rolling Piedmont terrain, so a lot of local decks step down a grade on tall posts rather than sitting close to level ground. Taller posts mean more leverage on the post-to-beam and post-to-footing connections, longer stair runs with stringers that often end on soil or a block instead of a frost-depth footing, and a guard height that is unambiguously in code territory. The code requires guards over 30 inches above grade, at least 36 inches high on a house, with no gap a 4-inch sphere can pass through. We measure all of that rather than guessing from the top of the deck.

Will you tell me if my Fallston deck is past repairing?

Yes, and that is the whole reason this business exists. Most companies you can call in Harford County build new decks, so a call about three soft boards tends to come back as a replacement quote. We repair decks. If the joists, beams, posts and ledger on your Fallston deck are sound, we fix what is wrong and leave the rest alone. If framing rot is widespread or the structure cannot be brought up to current code, we tell you that in writing instead of selling you a repair that will not hold.
