We repair decks throughout Columbia, Maryland — and unlike most deck companies working Howard County, we start the conversation with your village architectural committee rather than finding out about it halfway through. Every property in Columbia sits under recorded covenants, all decks require an application before construction, and approval is handled village by village rather than by one central office. That's the single most useful thing to understand before you spend a dollar on your deck here.

Columbia is a volume market, not a density one

Columbia has 104,681 residents but only 39.4% of its housing is detached single-family — a low share by suburban standards, and the reason some contractors write the town off. That reading is wrong. 39.4% of a place this size still works out to roughly 41,000 detached homes, which is more freestanding houses with backyard decks than most of the towns we serve have residents. Median household income sits around $136,002. The deck work is here; it's just spread across ten villages instead of concentrated in one subdivision.

Columbia's oldest housing dates from the late 1960s and the newest villages were still filling in through the 1990s, so the deck stock spans a wide age band. Practically, that means we see two distinct jobs: original decks on early-village homes that have reached the end of their framing life, and 1990s-and-later decks where the structure is fine but boards, railings and finish are worn out. We are a repair company, not a deck builder — we fix what can be fixed, and when a deck genuinely is past saving we say so instead of writing a replacement quote.

Village covenant approval: how it actually works

This is the part that catches homeowners out. Columbia's covenants are recorded against the property and administered by each village's architectural committee, so there is no single Columbia-wide deck rule to look up. What is consistent: all decks require an application before construction, and where more than one set of covenants applies to a property, the most restrictive one governs.

The Hickory Ridge process, step by step

Hickory Ridge publishes the clearest version of the sequence, and other villages run their own variation of it: submit an Exterior Alteration Application → a Covenant Advisor reviews it → the Resident Architectural Committee makes a property visit → the decision arrives by email, typically in two to four weeks. Hickory Ridge's Covenant Advisor can be reached at 410-730-7327.

What a committee wants to see. Wilde Lake's posted guidelines are the most detailed published example and a fair model for what to prepare: a site plan; a dimensioned drawing showing style, height above grade and railing and stair details; a description of materials and colors; a screening and landscaping drawing; and exterior lighting details. The standard applied is that the work must match the house in scale, materials, color and texture. A complete package is usually the difference between a clean approval and a decision that comes back with conditions.

If you're turned down. A village-board 15-Day Notice escalates the matter to the Columbia Association's Architectural Resource Committee. Knowing the route exists is worth more than most homeowners realise — it means a first "no" is not necessarily the end of the discussion.

Town Center is the exception worth knowing. Town Center currently operates a "Like for Like" streamlined review, which is designed for exactly the kind of replace-what's-there work a repair specialist does most.

One thing we won't guess at: whether re-staining your deck in its existing color requires an application. We could not verify a rule that holds across all ten villages, so we won't state it either way — call your village Covenant Advisor and ask before the work is scheduled. The ten villages

We work in all of them — Wilde Lake (1967), Harper's Choice (1968), Oakland Mills (1969), Long Reach (1971), Owen Brown (1972), Town Center (1974), Hickory Ridge (1974), Kings Contrivance (1977), Dorsey's Search (1980) and River Hill (1990). Founding dates matter here in a practical way: a Wilde Lake or Harper's Choice home has had far more time to accumulate deck generations than a River Hill home, and the older the original structure, the more likely the ledger, posts and footings — not the boards — are what actually need attention.

Howard County permits are a separate question

Covenant approval and a county permit are two different systems, and you can need both. Howard County's rules:

  • Only decks under 25 square feet are exempt — and there is no height exemption, so the 30-inch rule homeowners repeat does not apply here.
  • Board-only replacement is exempt as ordinary repair, so re-decking sound framing doesn't require a county permit. It may still require a village application.
  • Inspections run footing, framing, final, with footings to a 30-inch frost depth.
  • Howard is on the 2024 International Residential Code, effective September 7, 2025 — ahead of Baltimore, Anne Arundel, Harford and Carroll counties, which are on the 2021 family.
  • Staining, sealing and power washing are not addressed in the county's permit materials — which is not the same as "no permit needed."

Terrain, weather and what fails here

Columbia sits on rolling hills cut by stream valleys, with three artificial lakes and a lot of retained tree cover. On the valley-side lots that means tall decks over sloped yards and deep shade on at least one face. The area gets roughly 45 inches of rain a year — the sum of the National Weather Service's 1991–2020 monthly normals at BWI — humidity near 75% from August through October, and temperatures that swing above and below freezing repeatedly through the winter. Horizontal deck boards hold standing water while vertical faces shed it, and leaf debris blocks sunlight and compounds algae and mildew growth, so the shaded half of a Columbia deck ages noticeably faster than the sunny half. Deck Repair & Covenant FAQs for Columbia

Do I need village approval to repair my deck in Columbia?

Every property in Columbia is subject to recorded covenants, and all decks require an application before construction. Approval is handled by your village architectural committee, not by one central office, so the exact form and standard depend on which village you live in. Where more than one set of covenants applies to a property, the most restrictive one governs. Because the line between a repair and an alteration is drawn locally, the safest first move on anything beyond like-for-like work is a call to your village Covenant Advisor before we start.

How long does Columbia village architectural approval take?

Hickory Ridge publishes the clearest timeline: you submit an Exterior Alteration Application, a Covenant Advisor reviews it, the Resident Architectural Committee visits the property, and a decision comes by email — typically within two to four weeks. Hickory Ridge's Covenant Advisor can be reached at 410-730-7327. Other villages run their own version of the same sequence, so confirm the schedule with your own village office and plan the work around it rather than the other way round.

Does re-staining my deck in the same color need an application?

We are not going to guess at that one. Columbia's villages set their own standards for what counts as maintenance versus an exterior alteration, and we have not been able to verify a single rule that applies across all ten villages for re-staining in an existing color. Call your village Covenant Advisor and ask directly before the work is scheduled — it is a two-minute question that avoids a genuine problem. Separately, Howard County's permit materials do not address staining, sealing or power washing at all, which is a different question from the covenant one.

What does my village want to see in a deck application?

Wilde Lake's posted guidelines are a good model for what committees look for: a site plan, a dimensioned drawing showing style, height above grade and railing and stair details, a description of materials and colors, a screening and landscaping drawing, and exterior lighting details. The standard applied is that the work should match the house in scale, materials, color and texture. Assembling that package properly is usually what determines whether a decision comes back clean or with conditions.

Do I need a Howard County permit as well as village approval?

They are two separate systems and you can need both. Howard County exempts only decks under 25 square feet, with no height exemption at all, but treats board-only replacement as ordinary repair requiring no permit. The county inspects footing, framing and final, and works to the 2024 International Residential Code, adopted effective September 7, 2025 — ahead of the surrounding counties. Village covenant approval is separate from that and is required for decks regardless of what the county says about permits.
