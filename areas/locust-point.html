Locust Point rowhomes and the decks added on top

Locust Point's rowhomes began as early-1900s industrial worker housing — compact footprints, party walls, flat roofs, built beside a working waterfront. Median home values here now run roughly $420,572 to $520,000, among the strongest in the city's rowhome belt, and much of that value came from the renovation wave that swept these blocks through the 2000s and 2010s. A rooftop deck was a standard part of that package.

Which means the typical Locust Point roof deck is now 10 to 20-plus years old — the age at which the membrane, the flashing and the railings all come due at once. The deck may still look presentable from the hatch. What is happening between the boards and the roof is a different story.

The two-trades gap, and what falls into it

A rooftop deck is two assemblies stacked on each other, built by two different trades, warranted separately and inspected by nobody after the first year. Here is what we find living in the gap between them:

  • Penetrations. Every post base and curb that passes through the deck has to be flashed. An unflashed or badly flashed penetration is simultaneously a structural connection and a hole in the roof, and it leaks years before the membrane itself fails. This is the single most common source of a Locust Point ceiling stain.
  • The wrong waterproofing system. A standard roofing membrane installed where a pedestrian deck waterproofing system belongs cracks within a couple of seasons. It looks identical on day one and costs less, which is exactly why it keeps happening.
  • Membrane failure itself. Blistering, alligatoring and cracking from ultraviolet exposure and thermal movement — the number one rooftop deck failure overall. Baltimore contributes roughly 45 inches of rain a year (the sum of the National Weather Service's 1991–2020 monthly normals at BWI), July average highs near 88.8°F on an exposed surface, and a winter that swings above and below freezing repeatedly.
  • Sleepers and framing over the roof. With humidity near 75% from August through October and a freeze-free season of more than 230 days, wood held against a damp roof surface rots from the bottom up while the walking surface above it still feels solid.
  • Guards. Required where a walking surface is more than 30 inches above the level below, at least 36 inches high on a one- or two-family home, with no opening a 4-inch sphere can pass through — and fastened to something that is both structurally sound and properly flashed.

Timing is the whole game. Once a deck sits on a roof, replacing the membrane underneath requires removing the deck. Caught early, a Locust Point leak is a flashing repair at one post base. Caught late, it is a tear-off, a new membrane and a rebuild — plus whatever the water did to the framing on the way through.

The Baltimore City permit path, start to finish

Baltimore City is the strictest jurisdiction in the metro on decks, and the reason is simple: there is no exemption to fall under. Decks are absent from the city's list of work exempt from permit — no square footage, no height. Baltimore County at least publishes a threshold at 16 inches above grade or 120 square feet; the city publishes none. There is general ordinary-repairs language, but that is a narrow lane, not a size exemption.

For a Locust Point rooftop deck the path runs:

  1. Confirm historic district designation by address. If the address falls inside a CHAP district, CHAP approval comes before the building permit and the design constraints change substantially.
  2. Construction documents signed and sealed by an architect or engineer licensed in Maryland — mandatory for rooftop structures.
  3. Written notification to adjoining property owners, with evidence of that notice submitted alongside the documents. On a party-wall block, both neighbors.
  4. Design within the rooftop structure limits: no deck raised more than 2 feet above the plane midway between the lowest and highest points of the roof surface; 8 feet back from the face of the building wall on a flat roof, or 6 feet behind the ridge on a gabled or mansard roof; no side or rear setback required.
  5. City-performed inspections at footing and final.

Those height and setback numbers bind on repairs too, not just new decks — rebuilding slightly higher or pushing a deck forward for more usable area can make a previously conforming deck non-conforming. Our Baltimore City rooftop deck permit and CHAP guide covers the whole sequence.

Why the structure is not what your contractor assumes

A Baltimore rowhome rooftop deck typically spans party wall to party wall on shared masonry rather than hanging from a wood ledger bolted to a rim joist. Every prescriptive deck detail and span table in the residential code is written for the ledger arrangement. That mismatch is why the sealed drawings are required, and it is the practical reason a contractor who quotes your Locust Point roof deck at back-yard-deck prices is quoting the wrong job.

Rooftop Deck FAQs for Locust Point Homeowners

Should I call a roofer or a deck contractor about my Locust Point roof deck?

That question is exactly why these decks stay broken. A roofer will look at the membrane and decline to touch the framing sitting on it. A deck builder will look at the framing and decline to touch the roof. Meanwhile the leak lives at the seam between the two — usually a penetration where a post base passes through the assembly, or a flashing detail at a curb or a wall. We work that seam deliberately: we diagnose the membrane, the flashings and the penetrations together with the sleepers, framing and railings, and tell you which trade's problem it actually is before anybody starts removing boards.

What is the difference between a roofing membrane and a pedestrian deck waterproofing system?

A roofing membrane is designed to shed water on a surface nobody walks on. A pedestrian deck waterproofing system is designed to do that while also taking foot traffic, furniture and point loads. When a standard roofing membrane gets installed where a pedestrian system belongs — which happens often, because it is cheaper and looks the same on day one — it cracks within a couple of seasons. If your Locust Point deck was renovated on a budget and started leaking within a few years, that mismatch is one of the first things we check for.

What does the Baltimore City permit process look like for a Locust Point rooftop deck?

Start from the fact that there is no exemption to fall under: decks are absent from Baltimore City's list of work exempt from permit, so there is no size or height threshold like Baltimore County's 16 inches or 120 square feet. For a rooftop deck the sequence is construction documents signed and sealed by an architect or engineer licensed in Maryland; written notification to adjoining property owners with evidence of that notice submitted alongside the documents; the permit itself; then city-performed inspections at footing and final. If your block falls within a historic district, approval from CHAP has to come before the building permit — we confirm designation by address before anything else.

Is Locust Point in a historic district?

Designation is determined address by address, not by neighborhood name, so we check yours rather than guessing. The CHAP historic districts that most often affect rooftop deck work in this part of Baltimore are Fells Point, Federal Hill, Upper Fells Point and Mount Vernon. If an address does fall inside a district, the order of operations changes completely: CHAP approval comes before the building permit, no deck may be visible from the front of the structure, stair penthouses are discouraged in favor of internal stair or hatch access, and raw unpainted pressure-treated lumber is generally not approved.

What do you check on a Locust Point rooftop deck railing?

Height, infill spacing and — most importantly — what the posts are actually fastened to. Guards are required where a walking surface is more than 30 inches above the level below, must be at least 36 inches high on a one- or two-family home, and must not have any opening a 4-inch sphere can pass through. On a rooftop deck the attachment is the real question, because a post base that penetrates the waterproofing is simultaneously a structural connection and a hole in your roof. We check that the guard is solid and that the penetration under it is properly flashed, because on these decks those two problems are the same problem.
