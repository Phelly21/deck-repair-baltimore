- **Humidity ~60% Feb–Apr, ~75% Aug–Oct** (Maryland State Climatologist). ~16.8 muggy days in July.

- **Thunderstorm days: 30/yr** in eastern Maryland, peaking July.
- **DO NOT publish a freeze-thaw cycle count — no such figure exists.** Write "swings above and below
  freezing repeatedly through the winter."
- **Storms:** NOAA NCEI — Maryland had 85 billion-dollar disasters 1980–2024 (15 tropical cyclone, 38
  severe storm, 16 winter storm); recent-year average 5.6 events/yr vs 1.9/yr historically.
  Hurricane Isabel (Sept 2003): tide 8.34 ft above mean high water at Baltimore, ~34,000 MD homes
  impacted, just under $1B statewide. Sandy (2012): $42.6M statewide. Ida remnants (Sept 2021): 2–5"
  rain across northern MD.
- **Finish failure:** water-repellent sealers need reapplication every 12–18 months (sometimes 6–12
  under sun/traffic); semi-transparent/transparent stains 12–18 months on horizontals; solid-color
  1–3 years horizontal vs 3–7 vertical. Spring water-bead test.
- **Why the walking surface fails first:** horizontal boards hold standing water while vertical faces
  shed it; leaf and pine-needle debris blocks sunlight and compounds algae/mildew growth.
- **UV:** USDA Forest Products Laboratory documents wood is rapidly degraded by short-wavelength UV,
  with subsurface degradation from longer-wavelength violet/visible light.
- **PT southern yellow pine service life:** 10–15 years with maintenance; up to 15–25 above ground if
  every field cut is resealed; under 10 if never sealed. Treat as an industry range (Southern Pine
  Council), not a precise figure.
- **Woods common here:** pressure-treated southern yellow pine, western red cedar, Ipe, mahogany
  (red balau), composite. Trex is headquartered in Winchester, VA.
- **Composite caution:** Mahan v. Trex (2009) alleged pre-2009 composite decking was susceptible to
  mold, mildew, fungus, spotting and surface flaking; Trex denied the allegations and settled for
  $8.25M with product replacement plus partial labor reimbursement. Good "your 2005 composite deck is
  not maintenance-free" content. State it as an allegation + settlement, never as proven defect.
- **Where decks fail first (NADRA/InterNACHI):** ledger board, beams, support posts, hardware
  connections, railings, stairs. Ledgers are the most failure-prone, especially nailed rather than
  bolted. Code requires a 1-inch standoff above concrete for weather/splash-exposed post bases.
  Stair stringers often rest on soil or block instead of frost-depth footings.
- **Industry scale:** 60+ million decks in the US (50M residential, 10M commercial); NADRA estimates
  ~30 million are past their recommended service life and fewer than half of all decks are built to
  code. CPSC 2003–2007: 224,000 injured in deck/porch incidents, 33,000 from structural failure or
  collapse. May is Deck Safety Month (NADRA "Check Your Deck").
- **DO NOT USE:** "90% of deck collapses are ledger failures" or "~6,000 injuries/year" — untraceable.

### IRC points (2021 IRC basis — corroborated secondhand; phrase as "the code requires," avoid verbatim quotes)
- **R507.9.2 lateral loads:** 1,500 lb tension at each ledger end — either 2 hold-downs rated ≥1,500 lb
  within 24" of each deck end, or 4 rated ≥750 lb. In addition to ledger bolts, not instead of.
- **R507.9.1.3:** ledger fastener spacing by joist span (6'–18') and species, staggered rows.
- **R507.9.1.4:** continuous flashing at the ledger-to-house connection. (Cite R507.9.1.4 — the old
  R507.2.4 numbering was superseded.)
- **Prohibited ledger attachments:** cantilevered floor joists, **brick or stone veneer** (non-
  structural), engineered I-joist rim without manufacturer reinforcement. **The brick-veneer
  prohibition is high-value content for Baltimore's brick housing stock.**
- **R312.1 guards:** required over 30" above grade (measured within 36" horizontally of the edge);

- **R311.7 stairs:** max riser 7¾"; min tread 10"; handrail 34"–38"; handrail required at 4+ risers;
  graspable 1¼"–2" round.
- **Design loads:** 40 psf live + 10 psf dead.
- **R317.1.2:** wood within 6" of exposed ground or embedded in concrete must be ground-contact rated.

### Baltimore City rooftop decks — §1511 "Rooftop Structures" (VERIFIED, quotable)
- **Height:** no rooftop deck may be raised more than **2 feet** above the plane midway between the
  lowest and highest points of the roof surface.
- **Setbacks:** 8 ft from the building wall face on flat roofs; 6 ft behind the ridge on gabled/
  mansard roofs. No side/rear setback required.
- **Party-wall notification:** adjoining owners must be notified **in writing**, with evidence of
  notice submitted with the construction documents.
- **Professional seal:** construction documents must be signed and sealed by an architect or engineer
  licensed in Maryland.
- Inspections are city-performed (Inspection Scheduler / QuickTrac, 443-984-2776); footing + final.
- **CHAP historic districts** (Fells Point, Federal Hill, Upper Fells Point, Mount Vernon): CHAP
  approval required BEFORE the building permit. Rooftop decks are not banned but are often disallowed
  or strictly limited and reviewed for street visibility. **No deck may be visible from the front of
  the structure**; rear-alley-visible decks may be approvable. Rooftop stair penthouses ("doghouses")
  are discouraged; internal stair or hatch access preferred. Raw unpainted pressure-treated lumber is
  generally not approved.
- **Structural difference:** Baltimore rooftop decks typically span party wall to party wall on shared
  masonry, not a wood ledger on a rim joist — which is why the engineer/architect seal is mandatory
  and why generic deck contractors get these wrong.
- **What fails:** waterproofing membrane failure is #1 (blistering, alligatoring, UV/thermal cracking);
  every penetration (post bases, curbs) must be flashed; a standard roofing membrane used where a
  pedestrian deck waterproofing system belongs cracks within a couple of seasons; once a deck sits on
  a roof, replacing the membrane under it requires deck removal.
- [UNVERIFIED — DO NOT PUBLISH]: the 15.5" guard trigger, a horizontal cable-rail prohibition, and
  the "4–8 weeks CHAP timeline". Client must confirm with DHCD (443-984-1809) first.
- **No documented Baltimore rowhome rooftop-deck collapse exists — never imply one.**
